  - q: Do I need a paywall SDK to show a paywall in Compose?
    a: No. A paywall is a normal Compose screen. You only need a billing library (Google Play Billing, or a service like RevenueCat) to load prices and run the purchase.
  - q: Should prices be hard-coded in the paywall?
    a: No. Load the localized price from the store (for example ProductDetails.formattedPrice) and pass it to the UI. Hard-coded prices are wrong in other countries and currencies.
  - q: Where should the paywall live in my navigation?
    a: Treat it as its own destination (or a bottom sheet) that returns a result. Keep billing in a ViewModel or repository and give the screen only plans and callbacks.

**Short answer:** a Compose paywall is a screen with three jobs. It lets the user pick a plan,
starts the purchase for that plan, and shows the price, billing period, trial terms and renewal
information next to the button. Keep billing out of the UI: give the screen a list of plans and
callbacks, and run the purchase in your billing layer.

## The problem

Material 3 gives you cards, radio buttons and buttons, but no "paywall". Most first versions
miss something: a radio group TalkBack can't read, a price that ignores currency, or the
auto-renew line Google Play expects. The layout is the easy part; the details are what get
apps rejected or refunded.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Annual price first.** Show the real yearly price ($29.99 per year) as the main number and
  the monthly equivalent as a detail. Google Play lists "annual plans shown mainly as a
  monthly price" as a violation.
- **One screen, no surprise steps.** The price and renewal terms must be visible where the user
  taps Subscribe, not on a later screen.
- **Restore matters.** Users who reinstall or change phones look for it.
- **Test both themes and large fonts.** Make sure the disclosure still fits above the navigation bar.
